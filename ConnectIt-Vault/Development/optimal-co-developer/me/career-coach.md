## 8. Coaching Analysis — Recurring Themes

1. **You have a vision problem in reverse.** Most people lack a long-term vision; you have an unusually vivid one. Your gap is the *near field* — the next week, the next milestone, the next conversation. Every blocker you named touches this.

2. **Isolation is both a wound and a strategy — and right now it's winning.** The instinct to work alone is protective after being let go, but you've correctly diagnosed it as career-limiting. You don't need to trust "the industry." You need 3–5 specific individuals.

3. **Perfectionism is a symptom, not the disease.** It reliably appears *after* disorganization. Treat the disorganization and the perfectionism spiral shrinks on its own.

4. **Your strengths and your blockers are the same trait viewed from two sides.** Deep investment in the work → you connect powerfully with the right people and disconnect visibly from the wrong ones. Systems thinking → great architecture and analysis-paralysis perfectionism. Independence → you can solo a UE5 framework and you starve your network. The goal isn't to erase these; it's to add range.

5. **"Prove them wrong" is fuel with a short shelf life.** It's real energy now. But a studio built to spite people is fragile. Worth consciously migrating the motivation toward "build the thing I believe in" over the next year.

## 9. Working Action Framework

### Now → next 30 days

- [ ] **Ship the white-box demo.** Define "demo-complete" as a written checklist of ≤10 items. Nothing outside that list gets polished until the demo is done.
- [ ] **Weekly planning ritual (30 min, same time each week).** Answer three questions: What's the single next milestone? What are the 3–5 concrete tasks toward it this week? What will I *not* do this week? Write it where you'll see it daily.
- [ ] **Anti-perfectionism rule:** when you catch yourself refactoring/polishing, ask "is this on the demo checklist?" If no, note it in a "later" file and stop.
- [ ] **Job search cadence:** decide a fixed number of applications/week for pivot-friendly roles (technical designer, gameplay programmer, tools programmer, generalist at small studios). Track them here. Don't wait for the portfolio to be perfect — the demo-in-progress is a legitimate portfolio piece.

### Next 90 days

- [ ] **Rebuild a minimal network — 3 to 5 people, named.** Targets: former colleagues who were "invested in the work" (the ones who clicked with you), other solo/indie devs, one person ahead of you on the indie path. Low-pressure: share the white-box demo and ask for feedback. Collaboration on the project *is* networking.
- [ ] **Find one mentor-shaped relationship.** Doesn't need a title. Someone who has shipped an indie game or made the art→design/programming pivot. Communities: local IGDA chapter, indie Discords, r/gamedev, game jams. One conversation a month is enough to start.
- [ ] **Practice managing up deliberately** (relevant whether employed or leading collaborators): in any working relationship with someone you don't naturally click with, run an experiment — over-communicate status, ask what *they* need, treat it as a craft problem. Log what happens.
- [ ] **Confirm and record the practical constraints:** exact runway in months, salary floor, remote/location/visa. Vague runway is feeding vague urgency.

### Ongoing

- [ ] Reframe motivation checks: when energy drops, first question is "can I see my next concrete task?" If no → that's the problem, not motivation. Go back to the weekly plan.
- [ ] Every framework decision on the flagship: is this reusable value, or is this perfectionism wearing a reusability costume? Reusable = clean interface + tests. Perfectionism = polishing internals nobody will call yet.
