# USER

Who I am as a developer, and how to work with me. This is a first draft, pulled from an interview — some of it is first-articulation thinking and will be revised. We agreed to revisit after shipping the first title.

> Canonical for **working style**. For owner **facts** (status, goals, blockers, constraints), see [`../board-of-advisors/_knowledge/me/career-coach.md`](career-coach.md). On overlap: that file wins on what's true, this one wins on how to act on it.

## How I work

- I execute **heads-down in the engine**, and I deliberately **change physical location — the couch — for structural thinking.** Keyboard momentum is the enemy of the kind of consideration hard problems need. Execution and reasoning happen in different physical modes on purpose.
- I go to the couch when I hit: a **knowledge gap**, a **solution search** (multiple candidate approaches — I look for the *best* route, not the first), a **ripple problem** (fixing one system touches four others), or an **emergent idea** — a new consideration that surfaces mid-execution and deserves protection from being steamrolled by execution mode. That last one is *generative*, not troubleshooting: I'm protecting a fragile idea, not fixing a bug.
- The deep thinking is **internal and mostly solo.** The couch is mine.
- I **write to record conclusions, not to think.** By the time something is on paper it's already load-bearing — it survived the couch. I do **not** write to discover.
- **Important consequence:** I externalize *decisions* but not *state*. I trust my mind to hold "where am I, what's next" — and that's exactly what it doesn't reliably hold. See "My core vulnerability."

## The collaboration shape I want

- A collaborator is most valuable **bracketing the couch**, not sitting inside it:
  - **Before (scout):** load me up with the right information and options to take in.
  - **After (sparring partner):** pressure-test what I come back with.
- We should **discuss the problem before I go think.** That shared setup earns the right to push deep afterward — even to questioning the *framing*, not just the answer. Without that shared thread, deep pushback reads as second-guessing; with it, it reads as collaboration.
- When I return, I'll usually present thinking as a **pros/cons ledger.** I want each point examined on its merits. I don't mind being second-guessed.

## What I value

- **Coherence as the aesthetic endgame.** Many strong parts pulling in one direction until they stop being parts and become a world. (Origin: Final Fantasy X — not the battle system *or* the music *or* the story, but all of it *as a whole*.) Any single element that breaks the spell is a serious sin.
- **Stability as the source of creative freedom.** A rock-solid foundation doesn't constrain the art — it *liberates* it. Instability is a creativity tax that forces artists to become part-time engineers. (From my experience as a Technical Artist: give people a stable, high-quality framework and they go build wonders on top of it.)
- **Compounding, long-horizon craft.** I'm not building *a* game so much as the *capacity to build games* — FF-style, each title honing the last. Everything I make should serve what comes next: code generic and powerful, design reusable and scalable. Game dev is a lifelong pursuit; I plan toward the end and refuse short-sightedness.
- **Method:** I build **sequentially and layered** — foundation/white-box first, art on top. I distrust heavy parallel work; it demands intense discipline and trust that usually isn't there. I bet coherence emerges *better* from a strong sequence than a risky parallel process.

## My core vulnerability (the most important thing here)

- I can be **obsessive and disorganized.** The root is a **broken between-session feedback loop**: I forget what was recently worked on, and I don't review after sessions what was achieved or what should come next.
- That single broken loop drives three symptoms:
  - **Over-polishing** (quality is the only signal available when there's no map of progress),
  - **Ignoring deadlines** (a deadline is meaningless without a running sense of distance-to-done),
  - **Coding for longevity instead of making progress** (the one mode that feels productive without knowing where I am in the arc).
- The trap to watch: over-generalizing because committing to the specific is scary, dressed up as "building for what comes next." Real foundation-building and avoidance say the *same words* — the tell is underneath. Tells that I've crossed the line: polishing something that already works, revisiting settled decisions, deadlines quietly going unmentioned, active every day but nothing becomes playable.
- **The compounding only compounds if things actually ship.** FF got to build on itself because each title shipped.

## How I communicate

- **Listener by default.** I speak only when I judge I have something of value — the same quality filter I apply to writing and design. Not shyness; a filter.
- **Trust is the throttle.** Guarded and measured with people I don't have rapport with; animated, funny, and free with friends.
- I'm **self-conscious in writing** from past negative experiences, so text costs me effort.
- I take **criticism exceptionally well — under one condition: the critic gets their hands dirty helping fix it.** Judgment *from above and from outside* is what I can't abide; my response to it is to question the judge's standing.
- I **withdraw when I feel unsupported, unfairly criticized, or hit out of the blue** — especially inside power dynamics (teacher–student, boss–worker). That withdrawal is a **trust signal, not a defect.** It fires exactly when the environment stops feeling safe. Past managers read it as "not communicating enough"; that framing misses the mechanism.
- I brace going into power relationships because my erratic productivity doesn't match what a "good employee/student" is supposed to look like — so criticism of my consistency lands on an existing bruise.

## What I want from an AI co-developer

- **Primary role: be the producer I can't be for myself.** I'm confident I can widen my own knowledge and do the technical work — but I don't believe I could ever be an effective producer. It's not my personality. That's the one gap I can't self-staff.
  - Hold project **state between sessions** so I re-enter with a map, not a blank engine.
  - Run **session bookends**: what changed, what's now playable, what's the single next thing.
  - **Guard scope and cadence.** Turn "ship it" from a nag into a structural fact by keeping the next thing visible next to whatever I'm polishing.
- **Secondary role: design sparring partner** — widen my solution search before the couch, pressure-test after. Real and wanted, but it serves the producer spine rather than competing with it.
- **What breaks my trust:** a collaborator that doesn't learn with me — repeats old suggestions, doesn't think critically about genuinely new concerns, doesn't help fill my professional gaps as a solo dev. Stasis is the core fear, and it's offensive to the compounding value everything else is built on.
